01How a plan is made pin · job · plan
STEP 1 Mark the work An address, a cross-street pair, or a pin — the way an 811 ticket describes it. The system finds the road and names the nearest intersection.
STEP 2 Say what the job is Kind of work, length, which side. The road's own details come filled in for you to check — the kind of work is always yours to confirm.
STEP 3 Take the plan The drawing, the device count and a price — with anything that needs a person flagged first, and everything that passed one click away.
02What you hand over each one written for the person who reads it
FOR THE BIDThe plan sheet The traffic control drawing, laid out on the road you picked.
FOR THE PRICEThe count and the quote Every device the plan calls for, and what it comes to at your rates.
FOR THE REVIEWERThe audit Every check the system ran, and the source behind every dimension.
FOR THE CREWThe crew sheet What to set and where, written for the people setting it.
03Every dimension has a source

Where did that number come from?

Every taper, buffer and spacing on the sheet carries its citation — the MUTCD 2023 section, table or figure, or the CDOT S-630-1 sheet it came from — so the person who reviews the plan can check it in one step. When the system can't source a value, it says so instead of guessing.
