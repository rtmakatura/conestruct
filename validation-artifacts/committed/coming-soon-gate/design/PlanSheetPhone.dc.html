01How a plan is made
STEP 1Mark the workAn address, a cross-street pair, or a pin — the way an 811 ticket describes it. The system finds the road and names the nearest intersection.
STEP 2Say what the job isKind of work, length, which side. The road's own details come filled in for you to check — the kind of work is always yours to confirm.
STEP 3Take the planThe drawing, the device count and a price — with anything that needs a person flagged first, and everything that passed one click away.
02What you hand over
FOR THE BIDThe plan sheetThe traffic control drawing, laid out on the road you picked.
FOR THE PRICEThe count and the quoteEvery device the plan calls for, and what it comes to at your rates.
FOR THE REVIEWERThe auditEvery check the system ran, and the source behind every dimension.
FOR THE CREWThe crew sheetWhat to set and where, written for the people setting it.
03Every dimension has a source

Where did that number come from?

Every taper, buffer and spacing on the sheet carries its citation — the MUTCD 2023 section, table or figure, or the CDOT S-630-1 sheet it came from — so the person who reviews the plan can check it in one step. When the system can't source a value, it says so instead of guessing.
