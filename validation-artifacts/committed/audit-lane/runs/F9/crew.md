## Setup Procedure

**Important: Set up from downstream to upstream (against traffic flow). This ensures you are always working behind protection.**

1. Identify both flagger station positions (do not staff yet; stations are staffed after all devices are in place, step 7): Station #1 at station 1,060 ft (approach side, 100 ft upstream of the taper start, closed-lane side); Station #2 at station -300 ft (opposing approach, 300 ft past the work-area end per CDOT S-630-1 Case 17).

2. Place the END ROAD WORK sign (G20-2) at the downstream termination (station -150 ft), right side of road 16 ft from centerline. Place the opposing-direction G20-2 past the upstream end of the work zone (left side).

3. Place 4 traffic cones in the downstream taper, between station -50 ft (right lane edge) and station 0 (centerline) at 12 ft spacing. This taper returns right-direction traffic from the centerline back to the right lane edge past the work area (MUTCD §6B.08 ¶12; S-630-1 Case 17 annotates the run 100' MAX.). Then place 7 traffic cones along the centerline boundary between the open opposing lane and the closed work lane at 83 ft spacing, starting at station 0 (downstream end of work zone) and working upstream to station 500 ft. Then continue the line through the buffer space: 3 more traffic cones at 90 ft spacing, from station 500 ft up to the taper (station 860 ft), at the same offset. Keep the buffer itself clear of work and equipment (CDOT S-630-1 Sheet 2 General Note 18b: no more than twice the speed limit apart along the buffer space).

4. Place G20-5P WORK ZONE plaque(s) within the work zone at approximately 250 ft intervals.

5. Place 6 channelizing drums in the one-lane two-way taper, starting at station 860 ft and working upstream to station 960 ft. Space drums at 20 ft intervals (approximately 20 ft per MUTCD §6B.08. This taper stages stopped traffic behind the flagger; it must be short enough to not be mistaken for a transition). Drums transition from the right lane edge (12 ft) to the centerline (0 ft).

6. Place advance warning signs upstream of the taper:
   - W20-7 at 600 ft upstream of taper start (station 1,560 ft), right side.
   - W3-4 at 1,100 ft upstream of taper start (station 2,060 ft), right side.
   - W20-4 at 1,600 ft upstream of taper start (station 2,560 ft), right side.
   - W20-1 at 2,100 ft upstream of taper start (station 3,060 ft), right side.
   Repeat the series for the opposing approach (left side, negative stations; S-630-1 Case 17: "SIGN SEQUENCE IS THE SAME FOR OPPOSITE DIRECTION").

7. Staff both flagger stations (STOP/SLOW paddles, high-visibility apparel, radio or agreed hand-signal communication between stations). Verify each station is visible to approaching traffic from the full advance-warning distance and has an identified escape path. Begin the alternating one-way operation.

## Takedown Procedure

**Important: The one-lane operation ends before its warning signs come down. Flaggers control traffic until the roadway is fully restored to two-way operation.**

1. Complete work and clear all personnel and equipment from the work area.
2. Under continued flagger control, remove the work-zone cones and the taper drums (restoring the closed lane).
3. Cease the alternating one-way operation and release both flagger stations.
4. Remove advance warning signs on both approaches: W20-1 first, then W20-4, then W3-4, then W20-7.
5. Remove G20-5P plaques and the G20-1/G20-2 BEGIN/END ROAD WORK signs.

## Safety Notes

- All personnel in the work zone must wear ANSI Class 2 (daytime) or Class 3 (nighttime) high-visibility apparel.
- Maintain 360 ft longitudinal buffer space between the taper and the work area at all times.
- Maximum speed reduction per sign installation: 15 mph (CDOT S-630-1 Sheet 2 General Note 3).
- This plan was generated by MHT Tool. A certified Traffic Control Supervisor (TCS) must review, approve, and sign before field implementation.
- Reference: CDOT S-630-1, MUTCD 11th Edition Part 6, Colorado Supplement (effective January 18, 2026).

## Pedestrian and Bicycle Accommodation

- No pedestrian or bicycle facility is flagged as affected by this closure. If a sidewalk, crosswalk, shared-use path, or bike facility exists within the work zone in the field, stop and have the TCS update the plan before setup.
- The TCS verifies pedestrian and bicycle accommodation on site; where a facility is maintained, it must remain accessible and detectable per MUTCD standards.

## Emergency Access

- The roadway is reduced to one alternating lane; access through the closure is controlled at the flagger stations.
- The Traffic Control Supervisor completes and confirms the emergency access plan for this site and coordinates notification of police, fire, and emergency medical responder agencies prior to work (TCS duties per CDOT Standard Specifications 630.11).
- An emergency access plan is a required minimum element of the approved Method of Handling Traffic (CDOT 630.10(a)); this section is draft input for it, not the approved plan.
